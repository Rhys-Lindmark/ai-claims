## Evidence routes

- Aging and end-of-life care: randomized and systematic evidence on palliative care, hospice, goals-of-care conversations, function, symptom burden, caregiver outcomes, and site of death.
- Clinical fallibility: diagnostic-error reviews, surgical learning curves, safety reporting, uncertainty, teamwork, and the difference between vivid cases and representative rates.
- Quality improvement: WHO and hospital checklist trials, hand hygiene, implementation science, feedback, context, and evidence that intervention labels conceal variable execution.
- Diagnostic cognition: heuristics, premature closure, second opinions, structured reflection, decision support, and the mixed transfer of debiasing training.
- Overdiagnosis: screening trials, lead-time and length bias, incidental findings, disease-definition expansion, absolute risk, and mortality versus detection outcomes.
- Political economy of U.S. medicine: prices, billing, insurance, consolidation, administrative cost, pharmaceutical incentives, and international health-system comparisons.
- Medicalization and iatrogenesis: treatment harms, opportunity cost, social determinants, disability, and the line between legitimate institutional criticism and a generalized rejection of effective medicine.
- Cross-cultural care: interpreter evidence, shared decision-making, structural competency, trust, family authority, and the limits of drawing a general framework from one Hmong family's tragedy.
- Trauma: PTSD guidelines, neurobiology, memory, psychotherapy trials, EMDR, body-oriented treatments, neurofeedback, yoga, and explicit separation of established from preliminary interventions.

## Guardrails

- Do not treat a clinical anecdote, however vivid, as an effect estimate.
- Separate diagnosis rates, surrogate outcomes, disease-specific mortality, all-cause mortality, quality of life, and harms.
- Preserve heterogeneity across patient preference, prognosis, condition, setting, baseline risk, and implementation quality.
- Do not infer that institutional incentives explain every clinical decision or that documented iatrogenesis makes effective care net harmful.
- Do not publish trauma mechanisms or treatments as established when major guidelines judge evidence insufficient or condition-specific.
- Keep cultural humility distinct from stereotyping patients by group identity.
- Score exactly three central claims per book using the public four-state credit map and at least two independently useful sources per claim.
