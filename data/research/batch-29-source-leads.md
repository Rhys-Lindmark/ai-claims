## Evidence map

- Pattern-language criticism and validation: Springer *City, Territory and Architecture*, Architectural Research Quarterly, and the 2025 systematic review of validation methods.
- Adaptable buildings: 2021 critical review and 2024 systematic review of building adaptability, plus Smithsonian bibliographic context.
- Human factors and usability: Norman's revised text, HCI scholarship on affordance, and accessibility/usability standards.
- Modernism and postmodern criticism: Yale University Press, Society of Architectural Historians, JSAH, architectural-history reviews, and documented built examples.
- Domestic history: contemporary reviews and historical scholarship on privacy, domesticity, comfort, and home technology.
- Landscape history: UNESCO cultural-landscape guidance, professional-history sources, and contemporary criticism of the Jellicoes' global synthesis.
- Architecture and wellbeing: systematic reviews of built-environment, mental-health, and social-health evidence.

## Main uncertainty boundaries

- Aesthetic judgments cannot be scored as factual claims merely because they are influential.
- Canonical manifestos often document their own impact better than they prove their design prescriptions.
- Case selection and retrospective interpretation weaken universal histories of architecture and landscape.
- Built-environment wellbeing evidence is heterogeneous, heavily observational, and sensitive to social context.
- The book's original publication context must be distinguished from later influence and later empirical tests.
